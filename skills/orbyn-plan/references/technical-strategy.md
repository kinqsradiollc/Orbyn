# Turn cross-team direction into adoptable work

Use for a consequential initiative or shared platform investment, not every ticket.
Describe the observed product/engineering constraint, who bears it, and the result
that would justify investment. Separate required obligations from preferences.
Compare no change, a targeted correction and broader investment using evidence of
impact, ongoing ownership/cost, reversibility and opportunity cost. Label uncertain
estimates; a senior title, code age or a popular tool does not settle the decision.

Map affected teams and consumers, including an operational owner and a decision
owner. Record conflicting needs and the evidence that would resolve them. Prepare
concrete options within the user's scope; do not invent agreement, send unrequested
messages or treat an agent assignment as team approval. Escalate an unresolved
consequential trade-off to its actual decision owner while continuing independent
work. Architecture owns interfaces and migration mechanics.

Pilot one complete consumer path. For shared tools/platforms, specify the developer
experience, documentation/example, support owner, adoption cost and escape path.
Choose adoption and user-outcome measures, not just implementation counts. Name
who maintains the capability after the original author leaves. Sequence dependent
teams and contracts without pretending they share a release cycle.

Return a short decision with assumptions, alternatives, owners, first verifiable
unit, stop/revisit conditions and adoption/retirement evidence. Revisit it when
measurements or dependencies invalidate the assumption. Capture a useful lesson
at its owning boundary; do not turn each task into another governance process.

Example: three teams want one SDK but release independently. A generated contract
and one consumer pilot may be enough; mandate neither a platform rewrite nor a
flag day. Retain released-version support and prove adoption before deleting adapters.

Background: [Google large-scale changes](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book/html/ch22.html)
and [GitLab staff responsibilities](https://handbook.gitlab.com/job-description-library/engineering/development/backend/staff/).
This is a local synthesis, not a universal career ladder or company policy.
